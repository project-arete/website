Concepts

Understanding Core Arete Concepts

Arete organizes networks around nodes, context, and capabilities — business roles and real-world relationships instead of technical addresses. This page defines the terms that carry the architecture, for business leaders and technical stakeholders alike.

Node

The unit of participation

A Node is the foundational unit of the Arete network: any device, digital service, or asset that participates in a system — from sensors and gateways to cloud applications and business software. Each Node represents its capabilities and interests to the broader network by publishing what it can do and in what context.

Nodes are synchronized as twins between Constituent Systems and the Orchestrator, so their functionality and relationships stay current — enabling rapid, adaptable integration without manual configuration or fragile custom code.

Context

Where capabilities apply

Context describes the environment or scope in which a Node’s capabilities can be applied — a physical location such as a building or room, a business function, or any other operational domain. Context is not just a tag: it is the mechanism that enforces business rules and policies, so only appropriate connections happen.

HVAC controls link only to their floors; financial systems connect only under the right regulatory domain — reducing risk and strengthening compliance by construction.

Capability

What a Node offers or needs

A Capability is what a Node offers or needs within a given Context. Capabilities define precisely what service, data, or action a Node is ready to provide or consume — from sharing sensor data to processing transactions, managing access, or automating building services.

Because every interaction is standardized and governed by clear contracts attached to the right Context, integration is unambiguous and scales cleanly.

Connection

The live, governed link

Connections are the live, operational links between Nodes, established when compatible Capabilities in a shared Context are identified. Unlike static address-based links, Arete connections are orchestrated dynamically from business intent — and every one is auditable, manageable, and secured through built-in policy enforcement.

Connection Profile (CP)

The standardized contract

A Connection Profile — always named as cp:profile.name — is a standardized contract defining how two Nodes interact over a Connection. Each CP specifies the roles involved (provider and consumer), the terms of the exchange, and the expectations each side must fulfill.

CPs are domain-agnostic, open source, and reusable, which is what makes integration between vendors, products, and systems plug-and-play. Profiles are governed centrally in the CP registry.

Role

Provider or consumer

Every Node assumes a role in a Connection Profile: provider — offering a capability — or consumer — making use of it. This division brings clarity and accountability, reflecting real-world business partnerships and supply chains, with policy defined as needed. Either role can apply to any system, device, or service.

Orchestrator System

The control center of a realm

An Orchestrator System is the control center of an Arete Realm. It maintains real-time inventories of Nodes, enforces policies, and brokers Connections by matching compatible Capabilities and Contexts. The Orchestrator automates monitoring, synchronizing, and securing relationships — so organizations spend less time on integration plumbing and more on what their assets can do together.
